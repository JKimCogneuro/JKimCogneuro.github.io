About my research and background

Biography

I am a cognitive neuroscientist and Research Professor at the Behavioral Science Research Institute, Korea University, where I work with Dr. Hackjin Kim in the Laboratory of Social and Decision Neuroscience. I use neuroimaging to study the brain processes involved in learning, emotion, and decision-making.

I completed my PhD at Kangwon National University under the supervision of Dr. Eunjoo Kang, studying how the brain processes feedback during learning. At the Centre for Addiction and Mental Health (CAMH) in Toronto, I worked with Dr. Antonio Strafella as a postdoctoral fellow, studying resting-state brain network dynamics in Parkinson’s disease. My work there also included PET and fMRI studies of brain connectivity in relation to cognition and emotion.

My current work asks how feedback from others affects our judgments and decisions. I am also interested in interoception—how we perceive signals from within our bodies—and its role in decision-making. I study these questions using fMRI, behavioral tasks, and physiological recordings.

In daily-life studies, I use experience sampling methods (ESM) and wearable sensors to examine how self-esteem varies over time and how these changes relate to cardiac activity.

Research

The questions I study

01

Feedback learning & social decision-making

How does feedback change what we learn and choose? My doctoral work examined feedback-based learning. I now study how feedback from other people affects judgments and decisions.

Behavioral experiments · Task-based fMRI · Physiological recordings

02

Brain networks, cognition & emotion

My work at CAMH examined resting-state brain network dynamics in Parkinson’s disease. I also co-led a PET–MRI study of FAAH binding and amygdala connectivity, alongside collaborations on cognitive and emotional symptoms.

Resting-state fMRI · Network dynamics · PET–MRI

03

Interoception & self-esteem in daily life

I study how the perception of internal bodily signals relates to decision-making. In a related line of field research, I use experience sampling and wearable sensors to examine daily changes in self-esteem and their relationship with cardiac activity.

Interoception · Experience sampling (ESM) · Wearable sensing
